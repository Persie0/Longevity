# No-equipment / home program

This version assumes no weights or machines. A pull-up bar dramatically improves back training, but the program still works without one.

## Weekly structure

- **Mon — Full body A**
- **Wed — Full body B**
- **Fri — Full body A**
- Alternate A/B/A then B/A/B the next week.

Add walking or other cardio on most days.

## Full body A

1. Bulgarian split squat — **3 × 8–20/leg**, 1–2 RIR
2. Push-up — **3 × 6–20**, 1–2 RIR
3. Single-leg hip hinge — **3 × 8–15/leg**
4. Pike push-up — **2–3 × 6–15**
5. Sliding leg curl or hamstring walkout — **2–3 × 8–20**
6. Single-leg calf raise — **2–3 × 10–25**
7. Side plank — **2 × 20–60 s/side**

If a pull-up bar is available, add:

- Pull-ups/chin-ups — **3 × 4–12**

## Full body B

1. Reverse lunge or deep squat — **3 × 10–20**
2. Feet-elevated or deficit push-up — **3 × 6–20**
3. Single-leg glute bridge/hip thrust — **3 × 10–25**
4. Nordic curl progression or sliding curl — **2–3 × 5–15**
5. Prone Y/T/W or reverse snow angel — **2–3 × 10–20**
6. Bodyweight triceps extension against sturdy furniture — **2 × 8–20**
7. Reverse crunch or hollow-body hold — **2–3 sets**

If a secure row setup is available, add inverted rows — **3 × 6–15**.

## How to make bodyweight exercises harder

Progress one variable at a time:

1. More reps.
2. Slower eccentric / better control.
3. Greater ROM.
4. Pauses in difficult positions.
5. More difficult leverage.
6. Unilateral version.

Examples:

- Push-up → feet-elevated push-up → deficit push-up → archer push-up.
- Squat → split squat → Bulgarian split squat → assisted pistol squat.
- Glute bridge → single-leg bridge → feet-elevated single-leg hip thrust.
- Pike push-up → feet-elevated pike push-up → handstand-push-up progression.

## Effort

Most work sets should finish with **1–3 reps in reserve**. With high-repetition bodyweight exercises, getting reasonably close to failure matters because the load can be light.

## Rest

- Large movements: **90–180 s**
- Smaller/core movements: **60–120 s**

## Biggest limitation

Without anything to pull against, the back/lats are difficult to train through meaningful resistance. If possible, the single best small equipment addition is a **safe pull-up bar or suspension/row setup**.
