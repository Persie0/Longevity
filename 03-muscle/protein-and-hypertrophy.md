# Protein for modest muscle gain and long-term maintenance

## The main target

For a healthy adult doing resistance training:

- **Maintenance / active lifestyle:** roughly **1.2–1.6 g protein/kg/day**
- **Modest muscle gain:** target about **1.6 g/kg/day**
- Going much above ~1.6 g/kg/day usually gives little additional hypertrophy benefit when calories and training are already adequate.

Example at 75 kg:

- 1.2 g/kg = 90 g/day
- 1.6 g/kg = 120 g/day

## Meal distribution

A practical target is **0.3–0.4 g/kg per meal**, across 3–4 meals.

At 75 kg, that is roughly 23–30 g per meal; if using four meals to hit 1.6 g/kg/day, ~30 g per meal works well.

The evidence for perfectly even distribution is weaker than the evidence for adequate total daily protein, so do not obsess over exact timing.

## Protein quality and leucine

High-quality proteins generally provide enough essential amino acids and leucine to stimulate muscle protein synthesis.

Useful protein anchors:

- 150–180 g fish
- 150–200 g chicken/turkey
- 250 g skyr/Greek yogurt plus nuts/seeds
- 200–250 g tofu/tempeh
- 250 g cooked lentils/beans plus a grain
- eggs combined with yogurt, legumes or whole grains

A meal containing roughly **2–3 g leucine** and adequate total essential amino acids is a useful practical benchmark, but total daily protein matters more.

## Plant protein

Plant-based muscle gain works. The easiest high-quality choices are soy foods and blended proteins.

For lower-leucine plant meals:

- use a somewhat larger protein portion;
- combine legumes with grains/nuts/seeds across the day;
- consider soy, pea+rice blend, or a protein powder if needed.

## Energy surplus

For “a bit” of muscle gain, avoid an aggressive bulk. Use roughly **maintenance to a small surplus (~5–10%)**, then adjust based on body-weight trend, waist and gym performance.

If body fat is rising quickly, the surplus is too large.

## Carbohydrate

Carbohydrates support training quality and glycogen. Favor:

- oats
- rice
- potatoes
- whole-grain bread/pasta
- fruit
- legumes

There is no longevity advantage to forcing a very-low-carb diet if it reduces fruit, legumes, whole grains or training performance.

## Around training

A protein-containing meal within a few hours before or after lifting is sufficient. The “anabolic window” is much wider than the old 30-minute myth.

## Resistance training is the dominant signal

Protein supplements produce only modest additional gains compared with the effect of resistance training itself. Consistent progressive resistance exercise is the key stimulus.

## Older age

Protein needs often rise with age because of anabolic resistance. Healthy older adults commonly benefit from at least ~1.0–1.2 g/kg/day, with ~1.2–1.6 g/kg/day often used when active and trying to preserve muscle.

## Optional supplement: creatine

Creatine monohydrate is the most evidence-based non-protein supplement for strength and lean mass. A simple dose is **3–5 g/day**. It is optional, not a substitute for diet or training.

People with kidney disease or relevant medical concerns should discuss high protein/creatine intake with a clinician.
