# Best exercises by body part

There is rarely one universally best exercise. The strongest choices are movements that train the target muscle through a large comfortable range of motion, are stable enough to progress, and fit the person's joints and anatomy. EMG alone should not be used to crown a single exercise.

## Chest

### Gym / weights
- Barbell bench press
- Dumbbell bench press
- Incline dumbbell or barbell press
- Machine chest press
- Cable or pec-deck fly

### No equipment
- Push-ups
- Feet-elevated push-ups
- Deficit push-ups between sturdy supports
- Close-grip push-ups for more triceps emphasis

Best default: one horizontal press plus one incline or fly variation if hypertrophy is a priority.

## Upper/mid back and rear delts

### Gym / weights
- Chest-supported row
- One-arm dumbbell row
- Cable row
- Reverse pec deck / rear-delt fly
- Face pulls as an accessory

### No equipment
- Inverted row under a secure table/bar if available
- Prone Y/T/W raises
- Reverse snow angels

Rows are generally more important than small corrective movements for building the back.

## Lats

### Gym / weights
- Pull-ups/chin-ups
- Lat pulldown
- One-arm cable pulldown
- Dumbbell/cable row with elbow kept closer to torso

### No equipment
True lat training without something to pull against is limited. Best options are:
- Pull-ups on a safe bar
- Towel/body rows only if the anchor is genuinely secure
- Isometric lat contractions can maintain familiarity but are inferior to loaded pulling

If completely equipment-free, back training is the biggest limitation of home programs.

## Shoulders

### Front delts
Pressing already trains them strongly.
- Overhead press
- Dumbbell shoulder press
- Pike push-up / handstand-push-up progression

### Side delts
- Cable lateral raise
- Dumbbell lateral raise
- Machine lateral raise

No-equipment isolation is difficult; pike pressing provides some shoulder stimulus but not a perfect substitute for lateral raises.

### Rear delts
- Reverse pec deck
- Cable rear-delt fly
- Chest-supported rear-delt row
- Prone reverse fly / Y-T-W at home

## Biceps

### Gym / weights
- Incline dumbbell curl
- Cable curl with arm behind torso
- Preacher curl
- Hammer curl

### No equipment
- Chin-ups if a bar is available
- Self-resisted curls
- Towel curls against your own leg/foot resistance

Long-muscle-length curl variations are a good hypertrophy choice, but any curl that can be progressively loaded works.

## Triceps

### Gym / weights
- Overhead cable triceps extension
- Cable pressdown
- Close-grip bench press
- Dips if shoulders tolerate them

### No equipment
- Close-grip push-ups
- Diamond push-ups
- Bodyweight triceps extensions against a sturdy surface

Overhead extensions are useful because they train the long head at a longer muscle length.

## Forearms and grip

### Gym / weights
- Farmer carries
- Dead hangs
- Heavy rows/deadlifts
- Wrist flexion/extension if direct development is desired

### No equipment
- Dead hangs if a bar exists
- Towel wringing/squeezing
- Fingertip support progressions cautiously

Grip is strongly trainable through carries and pulling; direct wrist work is optional.

## Quadriceps

### Gym / weights
- Squat
- Hack squat
- Leg press
- Bulgarian split squat
- Leg extension

### No equipment
- Deep bodyweight squat
- Split squat
- Bulgarian split squat using a chair/bed
- Reverse lunge
- Step-up on a stable surface
- Assisted sissy squat progression

A squat pattern plus a unilateral movement is an excellent default.

## Hamstrings

### Gym / weights
- Romanian deadlift
- Seated leg curl
- Lying leg curl
- Good morning

### No equipment
- Sliding leg curl on a smooth floor
- Nordic curl progression with a safe anchor
- Single-leg hip hinge
- Hamstring walkouts

Use both a hip-hinge pattern and a knee-flexion pattern if possible.

## Glutes

### Gym / weights
- Deep squat/split squat
- Romanian deadlift
- Hip thrust/glute bridge
- Step-up
- Cable hip extension

### No equipment
- Bulgarian split squat
- Reverse lunge
- Single-leg glute bridge
- Single-leg hip thrust
- High step-up if safe

Squats/split squats train glutes very well; hip thrusts are useful but not mandatory.

## Calves

### Gym / weights
- Standing calf raise
- Seated calf raise
- Leg-press calf raise

### No equipment
- Single-leg calf raise on a step
- Bent-knee calf raise for more soleus emphasis

Use a deep controlled stretch at the bottom if comfortable.

## Hip abductors/adductors

### Gym / weights
- Hip abduction machine
- Cable abduction
- Adductor machine
- Copenhagen plank for adductors

### No equipment
- Side-lying hip abduction
- Lateral step-down
- Copenhagen plank
- Side plank variations

These muscles also receive meaningful work from split squats, lunges and single-leg training.

## Core: abs, obliques, trunk

The core's main functions include spinal flexion, resisting extension, resisting rotation, and transmitting force.

### Gym / weights
- Cable crunch
- Ab wheel rollout
- Hanging knee/leg raise
- Pallof press
- Farmer carry
- Back extension

### No equipment
- Plank
- Side plank
- Dead bug
- Hollow-body hold
- Reverse crunch
- V-up progression
- Bird dog

For general health, 2–4 exercises across the week covering flexion plus anti-extension/anti-rotation is plenty.

## Lower back / spinal erectors

### Gym / weights
- Romanian deadlift
- Deadlift
- Back extension
- Good morning

### No equipment
- Bird dog
- Hip hinge practice
- Superman in moderation
- Single-leg hip hinge

Heavy deadlifts are not required for lower-back health.

## Neck

Direct neck training is optional for most people.

If desired:
- Gentle isometrics in flexion/extension/lateral flexion
- Light harness/manual resistance only with conservative progression

Avoid aggressive loaded neck work without coaching, especially with a history of cervical symptoms.

# Best whole-body / compound movements

These give high return per unit time:

1. Squat or leg press
2. Hip hinge / Romanian deadlift
3. Horizontal press / push-up
4. Vertical pull / pull-up or pulldown
5. Horizontal row
6. Overhead press or pike push-up
7. Split squat/lunge
8. Loaded carry

A program built around 5–7 of these patterns can train nearly the entire body effectively.

# Do isolation exercises matter?

Yes, but mainly as accessories.

Compound lifts cover a lot of muscle mass efficiently. Isolation movements add value when:

- A muscle gets insufficient stimulus from compounds.
- Hypertrophy is a priority.
- Joint-friendly local volume is desired.
- A specific weak point matters.

For general longevity, compounds plus a few strategic isolations are enough.
