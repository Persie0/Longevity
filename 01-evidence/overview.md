# Evidence overview

## Bottom line

The most defensible diet for health and longevity is not a single branded diet but a **Mediterranean-style, minimally processed, plant-forward pattern** with:

- many vegetables and whole fruits;
- legumes most days;
- whole grains instead of refined grains;
- nuts and seeds daily or near-daily;
- extra-virgin olive oil and other unsaturated fats replacing butter/lard;
- fish/seafood regularly, especially oily low-mercury fish;
- optional fermented dairy such as yogurt/kefir;
- modest amounts of eggs and unprocessed poultry if desired;
- low intake of red meat and especially processed meat;
- minimal sugar-sweetened drinks, industrial trans fat, and heavily ultra-processed snack foods;
- little or no alcohol.

This pattern has support from randomized cardiovascular-outcome evidence (notably PREDIMED) plus large bodies of prospective cohort and meta-analytic evidence.

## Evidence strength

### Strong / high confidence

- Replacing saturated and trans fats with unsaturated fats improves cardiovascular risk.
- Whole grains are preferable to refined grains.
- High fibre intake is associated with lower cardiometabolic disease and mortality risk.
- Fruit and vegetable intake is strongly associated with lower mortality and chronic disease risk.
- Processed meat is best kept very low.
- Excess sodium raises blood pressure; keeping sodium moderate is beneficial.
- Alcohol is not required for health and carries dose-related cancer and other risks.
- Resistance training plus adequate protein is much more important for muscle than protein timing tricks.

### Moderate confidence

- Mediterranean dietary patterns reduce cardiovascular events in high-risk adults.
- Nuts, legumes, olive oil, fish and fermented dairy are useful recurring components of a healthy pattern.
- Plant-protein-heavy patterns are associated with better long-term outcomes than patterns dominated by processed/red meat.

### Plausible but less certain

- Fermented foods may improve some microbiome and immune markers, but evidence for lifespan extension is not established.
- Narrow eating windows, intermittent fasting, “autophagy diets”, glucose flattening, and other timing strategies may help some people control energy intake, but there is no strong evidence that a specific fasting schedule extends human lifespan independent of overall diet quality and energy balance.
- No individual food—berries, turmeric, cocoa, green tea, garlic, etc.—has evidence strong enough to override the importance of the total pattern.

## Practical daily framework

A simple plate structure works well:

- **1/2 plate:** non-starchy vegetables
- **1/4 plate:** protein source
- **1/4 plate:** whole grain, potato, or other minimally processed carbohydrate
- Add extra-virgin olive oil, nuts/seeds, herbs/spices
- Fruit as dessert/snack more often than sweets

For someone pursuing modest muscle gain, the protein quarter can be increased until the daily protein target is reached.

## Fibre

A good practical floor is **25–30 g/day**, with ~30–40 g/day often reasonable when tolerated. Increase gradually and drink enough fluid. Whole foods are preferred because they bring micronutrients and phytochemicals in addition to fibre.

## Sodium, sugar and alcohol

- WHO advises sodium below **2 g/day** (about 5 g salt/day) for the general population.
- Free sugars should stay below **10% of energy**, ideally lower.
- Industrial trans fats should be avoided.
- For longevity, alcohol should not be treated as protective. Less is safer; zero is a valid default.

## Energy balance

Longevity-oriented eating should avoid chronic overfeeding. For modest muscle gain, use only a small surplus and monitor waist/body-fat trend rather than aggressively bulking.
