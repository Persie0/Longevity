# Fats, dairy and fermented foods

## Fats and oils

### Best default: extra-virgin olive oil

Extra-virgin olive oil is the best-supported primary added fat in a Mediterranean-style diet. Use it for salads, sauces and ordinary stovetop cooking.

### Other good oils

- canola/rapeseed oil
- high-oleic sunflower oil
- other unsaturated plant oils

These are preferable to butter, lard and coconut oil when the goal is cardiovascular risk reduction.

### Nuts, seeds and avocado

Whole-food fat sources are excellent because they add fibre and micronutrients.

## Saturated fat

Keep saturated fat relatively low, especially by limiting:

- fatty processed meat
- large amounts of butter/ghee
- coconut/palm fat as dominant fats
- frequent pastries and fried packaged foods

The benefit comes mainly from **what replaces saturated fat**: unsaturated fats and high-fibre carbohydrate are good replacements.

## Dairy

Dairy appears broadly neutral to modestly favorable in many cohort/meta-analytic datasets, with product type mattering.

### Best practical choices

- plain Greek yogurt
- skyr
- plain yogurt
- kefir
- cottage cheese / quark

For muscle gain/maintenance, Greek yogurt/skyr/quark are especially convenient because they provide a lot of protein.

### Sweetened dairy

Prefer unsweetened or lightly sweetened products. Many flavored yogurts function more like desserts.

## Fermented foods

Evidence is promising but not strong enough to claim lifespan extension.

Good options:

- yogurt/kefir
- kimchi
- sauerkraut
- tempeh
- miso

Fermented vegetables can be high in sodium, so they should complement—not replace—fresh vegetables.

## Butter and cheese

Cheese can fit in moderate portions, particularly fermented varieties, but it is calorie-dense and can contribute substantial saturated fat and sodium. Butter is better treated as an occasional flavoring than the default cooking fat.
