# Vegetables and fruit

## Vegetables: best choices

There is no single “best” vegetable. The strongest strategy is **high variety across colors and botanical families**.

### Prioritize often

- **Leafy greens:** spinach, kale, chard, rocket/arugula, bok choy
- **Cruciferous:** broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage, Brussels sprouts, radish
- **Alliums:** onions, garlic, leeks, spring onions
- **Red/orange:** tomatoes, peppers, carrots, pumpkin, sweet potato
- **Mushrooms:** useful variety and micronutrients
- **Sea vegetables:** optional; nutritious but iodine can become excessive if large amounts are eaten daily

### Frequency

Aim for vegetables at **2–3 meals per day**. A useful practical target is roughly 3 vegetable servings plus 2 fruit servings daily, consistent with evidence that about five total servings/day is associated with lower mortality.

### Cooking

Mix raw and cooked vegetables. Cooking can improve availability of some compounds (for example carotenoids/lycopene) while reducing some heat-sensitive vitamins. Steaming, microwaving, roasting and sautéing are all fine.

## Fruit: best choices

Prefer **whole fruit**, not juice.

### Strong recurring choices

- berries
- apples and pears
- citrus
- kiwi
- stone fruit
- pomegranate
- grapes
- bananas

Berries are nutrient-dense, but they are not uniquely necessary. A cheap apple or orange is still an excellent choice.

### Juice and smoothies

Whole fruit is preferable because it is more satiating and preserves intact food structure. Small amounts of smoothie can fit, but drinking large amounts of fruit is an easy way to consume excess energy.

## Potatoes and starchy vegetables

Potatoes can absolutely fit a healthy diet, especially boiled/baked and minimally processed. They should be counted mainly as a **starchy carbohydrate**, not as a substitute for non-starchy vegetables.

## Frozen and canned produce

Frozen vegetables and berries are excellent. Canned vegetables/beans are fine; rinse when sodium is high. Canned fruit is best when packed in water/juice rather than syrup.
