# Whole grains, legumes, nuts and seeds

## Whole grains

Whole-grain intake is consistently associated with lower cardiovascular disease and mortality risk.

### Best recurring choices

- oats / steel-cut oats
- barley
- rye
- whole-wheat bread/pasta
- brown rice
- bulgur
- quinoa
- buckwheat

Quinoa and buckwheat are technically pseudocereals but fill the same practical role.

### What to look for

Prefer products where a whole grain is the first ingredient and fibre is meaningfully higher than in the refined alternative.

### Frequency

Use whole grains at **1–3 meals/day**, depending on energy needs and training volume.

## Legumes

Legumes provide protein, fermentable fibre, potassium, magnesium and slow-digesting carbohydrate.

### Best rotation

- lentils
- chickpeas
- black beans
- kidney beans
- white/cannellini beans
- peas
- soybeans/edamame

A serving of roughly **150–250 g cooked** works well in a main meal.

## Nuts

Nuts are repeatedly associated with favorable cardiovascular and mortality outcomes.

### Best choices

- walnuts: especially useful for ALA omega-3
- almonds
- pistachios
- hazelnuts
- peanuts (botanically legumes, nutritionally similar to nuts)

### Portion

About **20–30 g/day** is a practical default. Unsalted or lightly salted is preferable.

## Seeds

### High-value choices

- **ground flaxseed:** ALA omega-3 + lignans
- **chia:** ALA + soluble fibre
- pumpkin seeds: magnesium, zinc, protein
- sesame/tahini: useful unsaturated fats and minerals

A practical pattern is **1–2 tablespoons/day** of flax/chia plus other seeds as desired.

## Nut butters

Peanut, almond and other nut butters are fine when the ingredient list is mostly nuts. Portion size matters because they are energy-dense.
