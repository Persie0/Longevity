# Protein foods: best subgroups and trade-offs

## 1. Fish and seafood

For health + muscle, fish is one of the strongest animal-protein choices.

### Best recurring oily fish

**Top tier:**

- salmon
- sardines
- herring
- Atlantic mackerel / Pacific chub mackerel
- trout

These combine high-quality protein with EPA/DHA omega-3s and are generally low in mercury.

### Good lean seafood

- cod
- haddock
- pollock
- shrimp
- mussels
- oysters
- scallops

They are excellent protein sources, though generally lower in omega-3 than oily fish.

### Tuna: what type is best?

Tuna is convenient but not the best fish to rely on every day.

- **Canned light / skipjack:** lower mercury; FDA “Best Choice”. Good economical protein.
- **Albacore / white tuna:** substantially higher mercury; FDA “Good Choice”, so eat less often.
- **Yellowfin:** also higher mercury than canned light.
- **Bigeye tuna:** high mercury; FDA lists it among choices to avoid for mercury-sensitive groups.

For routine longevity eating, salmon/sardines/herring/trout usually beat tuna because they provide more omega-3 with less mercury concern.

### Frequency

A good default is **2–3 seafood meals/week**, with at least 1–2 being oily low-mercury fish.

## 2. Legumes and soy

These should be major recurring protein sources.

### Best choices

- lentils
- chickpeas
- black/kidney/white beans
- peas
- edamame
- tofu
- tempeh
- minimally processed soy foods

Soy is particularly useful for muscle because its amino-acid profile is better than many single plant proteins.

### Frequency

Aim for legumes/soy **most days** if tolerated.

## 3. Eggs

Eggs are nutrient-dense, convenient and high-quality protein. They are not mandatory and should not displace legumes, fish, nuts and whole grains.

A practical default for a healthy adult is **0–1 egg/day on average**, adjusted to the rest of the diet and blood-lipid response.

## 4. Poultry

Unprocessed chicken/turkey can be used as a lean protein source. Prefer minimally processed forms over nuggets, sausages or cured deli meats.

Use it as an optional rotation food rather than the center of every meal.

## 5. Red meat

Red meat supplies iron, zinc, B12 and high-quality protein, but higher intakes—especially when it displaces plant foods/fish—are not ideal for longevity.

A conservative longevity-oriented default is **0–2 servings/week**, choosing lean, unprocessed cuts.

## 6. Processed meat

Bacon, salami, sausages, hot dogs and many cured deli meats should be **rare or near-zero**. This is one of the clearest food-group reductions to make.

## 7. Protein powders

Not required, but useful when food alone makes the protein target inconvenient.

Best options:

- whey or milk-protein isolate
- soy isolate
- blended pea + rice protein

Choose products with minimal added sugar and credible third-party testing where possible.
