# References and evidence base

This repository prioritizes randomized trials, systematic reviews/meta-analyses, major clinical/sports-nutrition guidelines and official safety resources. Observational nutrition and lifestyle evidence is useful for dose-response and long-term pattern design but does not prove causation on its own.

## Interpretation notes

- A statistically significant biomarker change is not automatically a clinically meaningful benefit.
- “Raises NAD+,” “reduces CRP,” “improves antioxidant capacity,” or “changes the microbiome” does not prove longer lifespan.
- Creatine has strong evidence for performance/lean mass outcomes, not for extending lifespan in healthy humans.
- Protein powder is a convenience food/supplement; benefit depends on whether it helps achieve adequate total protein.
- Vitamin/mineral supplements are most defensible when correcting inadequate intake, deficiency or a life-stage/clinical need.
- Generic high-dose antioxidant supplementation has not produced broad longevity benefits and may be harmful in specific populations.
- Fish/seafood is preferred over assuming routine fish-oil capsules reproduce all food-associated benefits.
- Probiotics are strain- and indication-specific; there is no generic “best probiotic” for healthy longevity.
- Current human evidence does **not** establish NMN, NR, resveratrol, spermidine, fisetin, quercetin or other marketed geroprotectors as lifespan-extending supplements.
