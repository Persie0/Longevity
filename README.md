# Longevity

Evidence-based notes focused on **long-term health, cardiometabolic risk reduction, preservation of physical and cognitive function, and modest muscle gain/maintenance**.

Last major evidence update: **2026-09-04**.

This repository is deliberately conservative. It prioritizes randomized trials, systematic reviews/meta-analyses, major guidelines and clinically meaningful outcomes. Observational associations, mechanistic plausibility and biomarker changes are labeled as such rather than being presented as proof of lifespan extension.

## Executive summary

For a generally healthy adult, the highest-confidence approach is not a large supplement stack. It is:

1. A Mediterranean-style, minimally processed, plant-forward dietary pattern.
2. Adequate energy without chronic overfeeding.
3. High-quality protein, approximately **1.2–1.6 g/kg/day** for an active adult and around **1.6 g/kg/day** when modest hypertrophy is a priority.
4. Resistance training **2–4 days/week** plus aerobic exercise and high daily movement.
5. At least the WHO aerobic target: **150–300 min/week moderate** or **75–150 min/week vigorous**, or an equivalent mix, plus strengthening on ≥2 days/week.
6. Regular sleep and management of major clinical risk factors such as blood pressure, LDL/ApoB, smoking, obesity and diabetes when relevant.
7. Supplements only when they solve a real problem: a dietary gap/deficiency, a specific sport-performance goal, or a clinical indication.

### The supplement bottom line

For a healthy resistance-training adult eating well, the most defensible default is usually:

- **Creatine monohydrate 3–5 g/day** if strength/lean-mass performance matters.
- **Protein powder only as needed** to reach the daily protein target conveniently.
- **Psyllium** when dietary fibre is inadequate, constipation is an issue, or an additional LDL-lowering tool is useful.
- **Caffeine** as an optional acute performance aid, not as a health requirement.
- **Vitamin/mineral supplements only when diet, life stage, medications, blood tests or a clinician-identified risk justify them** (for example B12 in vegan diets, iron deficiency, inadequate iodine intake, calcium intake gaps, or vitamin D in selected contexts).
- Fish/seafood is preferred to routine high-dose fish-oil supplementation for most healthy people.

There is currently **no dietary supplement with convincing randomized evidence that it extends lifespan in healthy humans**. Changes in NAD+, inflammatory markers, mitochondrial biomarkers, glucose, testosterone, antioxidant capacity or epigenetic measures are not equivalent to demonstrated longer life or fewer major clinical events.

## Evidence grading used in this repository

See `01-evidence/evidence-grading.md` for the detailed framework.

- **A — Strong / useful:** reproducible benefit for a defined outcome with good evidence; benefit generally outweighs risk when used correctly.
- **B — Situation-specific:** useful for a deficiency, life stage, clinical context or specific sport demand, but not a universal daily supplement.
- **C — Promising / uncertain:** credible human signals, but trials are small, short, inconsistent or focus on surrogate outcomes.
- **D — Little practical value:** no consistent clinically meaningful benefit for the stated use, or redundant when diet/protein/training are already adequate.
- **E — Avoid / high-risk use:** poor risk-benefit ratio, meaningful toxicity/adulteration risk, or evidence of harm in common use cases.

## Core food pattern

A robust default is:

- vegetables at lunch and dinner and often at breakfast;
- around **2 fruit + 3 vegetable servings/day** as a practical floor consistent with large prospective evidence;
- legumes/soy most days;
- whole grains routinely instead of refined grains;
- nuts ~20–30 g/day and seeds regularly;
- extra-virgin olive oil and other unsaturated fats as primary added fats;
- fish/seafood 2–3 times/week, emphasizing low-mercury oily fish;
- optional plain yogurt/kefir/skyr and other minimally sweetened fermented dairy;
- processed meat near zero and red meat modest;
- sugary drinks rare/absent;
- industrial trans fats avoided;
- alcohol ideally absent or infrequent; it should not be consumed for presumed health benefit.

## Core training pattern

A robust default is:

- resistance training 2–4 days/week;
- each major muscle trained about 2×/week when practical;
- start around 6–12 hard sets per muscle/week and adjust to goals/recovery;
- most hypertrophy work roughly 5–30 reps/set, with 6–15 a practical center;
- most sets at roughly 1–3 reps in reserve; failure is not required;
- progressive overload by load, reps, ROM/control or volume;
- aerobic work 150–300 min/week moderate-equivalent, with some vigorous work if desired and tolerated;
- daily walking/movement, with **~7,000 steps/day** a useful evidence-based benchmark rather than a magic threshold.

## Scope and safety

This is an evidence synthesis, not individualized medical care. Pregnancy, kidney or liver disease, cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension, thyroid disease, diabetes medications, anticoagulants, malabsorption, eating disorders, osteoporosis, anemia, inherited metabolic disease and other clinical conditions can materially change diet, supplement and exercise recommendations.

For supplements especially, do not assume that “natural” means safe. In the US, supplements are not FDA-approved for safety and effectiveness before sale; other jurisdictions also differ in premarket oversight. Prefer single-ingredient products with transparent dosing and credible third-party quality testing.
