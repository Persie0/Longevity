# Longevity

Evidence-based notes focused on **long-term health, cardiometabolic risk reduction, preserving physical function, and modest muscle gain/maintenance**.

This repository is organized around practical decisions rather than “superfoods”, influencer claims, or one supposedly perfect exercise. The overall approach combines a Mediterranean-style, plant-forward diet with resistance training, aerobic exercise, daily movement, sleep/recovery, and conservative progression.

## Core idea

For a generally healthy adult, the best-supported combined pattern is:

1. Base most meals on vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, nuts/seeds and unsaturated fats.
2. Use extra-virgin olive oil as the main culinary fat.
3. Eat fish/seafood regularly, especially low-mercury oily fish.
4. Prefer legumes, soy, fish and fermented dairy as recurring protein sources.
5. Keep processed meat near zero and keep red meat modest.
6. For modest hypertrophy, aim around **1.6 g protein/kg/day**, with resistance training doing most of the actual muscle-building work.
7. Resistance-train **2–4 days/week**, with each major muscle trained around **2×/week** when practical.
8. Most hypertrophy work can be done across a wide rep range; **~6–15 reps** is usually the most practical, with most sets ending around **1–3 reps in reserve**.
9. Accumulate **150–300 min/week moderate aerobic activity or 75–150 min/week vigorous activity, or an equivalent mixture**, plus daily walking/movement.
10. For longevity, **cardio + strength is better than choosing only one**.
11. Keep sedentary time low, progress gradually, and sleep consistently.
12. Avoid treating supplements, fasting, special rep schemes, EMG rankings, or individual “longevity hacks” as proven lifespan interventions.

## Important scope note

This is a general evidence synthesis, not individualized medical care. Cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension, kidney disease, pregnancy, eating disorders, diabetes medication, major gastrointestinal disease, serious injuries, neurological symptoms, severe hyperlipidaemia, or other clinical conditions can materially change the appropriate plan.
